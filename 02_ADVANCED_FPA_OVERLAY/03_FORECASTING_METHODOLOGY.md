# FP&A Forecasting Methodology

## Core principle
Forecasts must be driver-based, traceable and consistent with approved management assumptions.

## Period classification
Always distinguish:
- Actual
- Budget
- AOP
- Forecast
- Latest Estimate
- Prior Year Actual

Never mix these categories without explicitly labeling them.

## Quarterly and YTD logic
Quarterly growth and YTD growth are different calculations.

Quarterly comparison:
Current quarter ÷ prior-year same quarter - 1

YTD comparison:
Current YTD ÷ prior-year same YTD - 1

Do not substitute one for the other.

## Five-percent methodology
Where the approved business instruction is a 5% growth rule, apply it exactly and consistently.

Base rule:
Current period = prior-year comparable period × (1 + 5%) + approved incremental/new-store contribution where applicable.

Do not invent a different growth rate merely because another formula produces a closer total.

## NSO / new-store logic
New stores must be treated according to the approved opening date and contribution methodology.

Do not:
- double count NSO revenue;
- apply NSO revenue before the approved opening period;
- apply LFL growth to the same NSO contribution twice.

Where NSO chaining is specified, preserve the approved chain across comparable periods.

## Revenue
Use the specified revenue driver for each line. If the instruction is same-quarter prior-year growth, use the same quarter prior year rather than a generic annual growth rate.

## Costs
Classify costs as appropriate:
- % of sales;
- fixed-cost growth;
- volume-driven;
- inflation-driven;
- headcount-driven;
- contract-driven;
- other approved driver.

Delivery cost, where specified, should be driven from delivery sales rather than total sales.

## Fixed costs
Where management specifies value-based growth, calculate the growth from the same quarter in the prior year rather than applying an arbitrary percentage of sales.

## Forecast validation
After forecasting:
1. Compare quarter-on-quarter where relevant.
2. Compare same quarter prior year.
3. Compare YTD.
4. Compare FY.
5. Check that totals reconcile.
6. Check that assumptions are actually flowing through formulas.
7. Explain material movements.

If the requested rule conflicts with an existing workbook formula, flag the conflict instead of silently choosing one.
